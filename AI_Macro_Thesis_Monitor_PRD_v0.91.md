# AI / Macro Thesis Monitor - Product Requirements Document (PRD)
**Version:** 0.91  
**Date:** October 4, 2026  
**Status:** Pre-build PRD - approved concept, ready for implementation
This document is the implementation handoff for a human engineering team or an LLM/agent system. It captures the product rationale, approved information architecture, data model, formulas, source strategy, cost gates, phased roadmap, success criteria, phase-by-phase verification loop, and the inter-agent coordination model. It is intended to be sufficient for a new builder to begin without rediscovering prior product decisions or relying on private chat history from another agent.
**Product safety boundary:** This is a research and decision-support dashboard. It is not investment advice and it does not execute trades. The product should make evidence, calculations, assumptions and uncertainty more visible - not replace user judgment.
## 1. Executive summary
The AI / Macro Thesis Monitor is a public-data-first financial research dashboard designed to answer a practical question: are the economic and financial conditions surrounding the U.S. AI investment cycle strengthening or weakening, and what observable evidence changed? The product begins with two hypotheses that surfaced the problem - Jim Paulsen's yield-curve/technology-earnings warning and Steve Eisman's AI price-war/capital-intensity/concentration concerns - but it is deliberately not built to prove either person right.
The dashboard tracks seven independent variables: (1) AI Economics & Competition, (2) AI Capital, Credit & Concentration, (3) Rates, Credit & Liquidity, (4) Fiscal Position, (5) AI Productivity Diffusion, (6) Energy, and (7) Labor. Each variable combines primary economic/company data, relevant market instruments, an explicit calculation layer, and a concise interpretation. The product also maintains a separate evidence ledger so filings, government releases, company announcements and news can be evaluated as verified facts, estimates, or claims.
The MVP is free-first. The proposed stack is Python + Streamlit + DuckDB + Parquet + GitHub/GitHub Actions. Official public sources - Treasury, FRED/ALFRED, SEC EDGAR, BEA, BLS, Census BTOS and EIA - form the reliable core. Paid real-time market data or premium news feeds are not required for MVP and cannot be connected without explicit approval.
## 2. Why this product exists
The core problem is not a lack of financial information; it is the difficulty of connecting market prices, public financial statements, macroeconomic data and narrative claims into a transparent short-term decision framework. Traditional market dashboards tend to show prices without explaining the economic mechanism. Commentary often explains a mechanism without showing the current data. This product joins both layers and exposes the work.
The key design insight is that contradictory evidence is valuable. Falling AI token prices can indicate commoditization pressure while simultaneously supporting faster adoption. A flattening 2s10s curve can weaken one historical warning while a rising 10-year real yield still raises the discount rate on long-duration investment. Strong AI adoption can coexist with soft labor data. The dashboard must preserve these tensions rather than average them into one score.
### 2.1 Starting hypotheses - lenses, not truths
- **Paulsen lens:** a steepening long/short Treasury curve has historically preceded periods in which Technology and Communication Services lose relative earnings leadership, with an approximate 18-month horizon in the source article.
- **Eisman lens:** AI model competition, falling token prices, open-weight substitution, concentration in a small number of buyers, capital intensity and increasingly complex financing can weaken the economics supporting the AI infrastructure cycle.
- **Counter-thesis:** cheaper AI can increase adoption and token volume enough to expand the total market; productivity gains can accrue to AI users even if model-provider margins compress.
- **Combined macro thesis:** the most important 2-5 year risk is not simply "AI fails." It is a mismatch between the returns generated by AI and the capital, energy and financing commitments made to produce them, especially when long-term rates, federal financing needs, energy constraints or labor disruption reduce the system's margin for error.

## 3. Product goals and non-goals
### 3.1 Goals
- Provide a fast, auditable view of the seven variables and how each has changed over relevant short-term windows.
- Place relevant market prices next to the underlying variable so users can compare fundamentals with market confirmation.
- Explain every calculation, input, source, unit, timestamp and revision state.
- Give each variable a 1-3 sentence plain-language interpretation focused on practical meaning, not prediction theater.
- Prioritize primary sources and make source quality visible.
- Retain revisions/vintages so historical analysis reflects what was actually known at the time.
- Use a free-first architecture that can later migrate to a VPS/managed database without redesigning the product.
- Enable a human or agent to build, test, fix and verify each phase against explicit acceptance gates.
- Make routine Work <-> Codex / coordinator <-> implementer handoffs repo-resident so the owner is never required to act as a manual copy/paste message bus between agents.

### 3.2 Non-goals
- No automated buy/sell signals or trade execution in MVP.
- No single master "AI risk score" or "market crash score."
- No assumption that correlation implies causation.
- No claim that one interview threshold or personality-specific level is authoritative; specifically, do not implement an "Eisman watch level" marker.
- No paid Bloomberg/Reuters/exchange-quality data dependency in MVP unless separately approved.
- No attempt to reproduce full copyrighted news articles.
- No attempt to forecast every macro variable; the product is a structured evidence monitor.

## 4. Product principles
| Principle | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Show the work | Every conclusion must be inspectable down to source inputs and formula. |
| Primary evidence first | Government data and SEC/company filings outrank commentary. Secondary estimates remain useful but clearly labeled. |
| Contradictions are signal | Mixed evidence should be displayed as mixed, not averaged away. |
| Market confirmation belongs with the variable | Relevant tickers/instruments are embedded inside each panel rather than isolated on a separate market page. |
| Interpretation must be practical | Every panel explains what changed, why it matters and what would confirm/refute the signal. |
| Free-first, approval-gated cost | No paid source or hosting upgrade without explicit approval. |
| Reproducible by default | A new human/agent should be able to clone the repo, load approved secrets and rebuild the product. |
| Vintage-aware research | Backtests and historical screenshots must use then-known data when sources revise. |
| Repository is shared agent state | Requirements, active task, phase status, implementation results and verification artifacts must be persisted in the repo or GitHub artifacts; agents must not depend on each other's private chat history. |

## 5. Target user and core workflows
Primary target: an engaged individual investor/researcher who understands basic markets but wants a disciplined way to test macro/AI narratives against public data. Secondary target: analysts, journalists, portfolio researchers, or AI agents that need an auditable evidence framework.
| Workflow | Expected experience |
| --- | --- |
| Morning / pre-market check | Open Dashboard, read Current Market & Macro Regime, scan seven interpretations, expand any changed calculation, then inspect Recent Evidence & News. |
| Event analysis | After an earnings report, Treasury auction, Fed move, oil shock or AI model price cut, see which variables changed and whether market prices confirm. |
| Thesis review | Filter Evidence by Paulsen/Eisman/Combined view to inspect supporting and counter evidence over a selected period. |
| Source verification | Click a metric/evidence item to reach the filing, API/source page, formula and timestamp. |
| Historical review | Reconstruct a prior date using vintage-aware data to evaluate whether an apparent signal was knowable at the time. |

## 6. Approved information architecture
- **Dashboard:** Current Regime strip; seven variable panels; Market Performance section; Recent Evidence & News; Thesis Summary / Paulsen x Eisman framing.
- **Evidence:** searchable/filterable evidence ledger with source quality, impact, hypothesis target, rationale, links and timestamps.
- **About:** why the dashboard was built, the Paulsen and Eisman source hypotheses, the combined framework, limitations, and direct source links.

## 8. Dashboard variable specifications
### V1 - AI Economics & Competition
**Core question:** Is AI intelligence getting cheaper faster than economically useful demand is growing?
**Purpose:** Test the Eisman-style commoditization / price-war thesis without assuming that lower token prices are automatically bearish.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| Comparable AI workload price index | Cost of a fixed workload: 1,000,000 input tokens + 250,000 output tokens for a maintained basket of comparable models; each model and basket are also shown separately. Index = current basket cost / base-date basket cost x 100. |
| Observed token volume index | Observed tokens from approved public usage proxies, normalized to 100 at the base date. Always labeled as sample/proxy, never market-wide volume. |
| Open-weight share | Open-weight observed tokens / total observed tokens x 100. |
| Frontier/proprietary share | Approved frontier/proprietary observed tokens / total observed tokens x 100. |
| Demand elasticity proxy | % change in observed token volume / absolute % decline in effective workload price over the same interval. Optional and explicitly labeled as a proxy because model mix and workload quality change. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** NVDA, AMD, AVGO, GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN, IGV
**Primary sources:** Provider price pages, approved public usage proxies (e.g., OpenRouter/Vercel where terms allow), company disclosures, Census/Ramp-style enterprise indicators when publicly usable.
**Interpretation requirement:** 1-3 sentences: explain whether price pressure is being offset by volume/adoption and whether model mix is shifting toward lower-cost/open-weight alternatives. Never conclude that falling token prices alone are bearish.
### V2 - AI Capital, Credit & Concentration
**Core question:** Are AI infrastructure commitments producing enough cash flow and diversified demand to justify the capital and financing burden?
**Purpose:** Track the financial transmission mechanism that could connect weak AI economics to hyperscaler cash flow, corporate credit, infrastructure suppliers and market concentration.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| TTM capex | Sum of the most recent four quarters of purchases of property/equipment or the company-specific capex definition; store raw XBRL tag and reconciliation. |
| TTM free cash flow | TTM operating cash flow - TTM capex. Use the same capex definition consistently and show the components. |
| Capex growth | Current TTM capex / prior-year TTM capex - 1. |
| Capex coverage | TTM operating cash flow / TTM capex. |
| FCF margin | TTM free cash flow / TTM revenue. |
| Interest coverage | TTM EBIT / TTM interest expense where both are comparable; otherwise display N/A with explanation. |
| Debt change | Current total debt - prior-year total debt; separately display disclosed new debt issuance when identifiable. |
| Concentration | Disclosed customer concentration metrics only. If a percentage is only a secondary estimate, mark it ESTIMATE and do not blend into a verified company calculation. |
| Off-balance-sheet exposure | Disclosed lease commitments, guarantees, VIE/SPV maximum loss exposure and similar items. Store source filing section/page and whether standardized XBRL is available. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, ORCL, NVDA, AVGO, AMD
**Primary sources:** SEC EDGAR 10-Q/10-K/8-K, company investor relations, ratings/credit data available from approved public sources, manually curated filing disclosures for non-standard items.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether capex is accelerating faster than cash generation, whether financing is becoming more debt-dependent, and whether customer/off-balance-sheet concentration is increasing. Separate verified filings from analyst estimates.
### V3 - Rates, Credit & Liquidity
**Core question:** Is the cost and availability of capital becoming more restrictive for long-duration equities, AI infrastructure and the broader economy?
**Purpose:** Track Paulsen-related curve behavior while avoiding the mistake of treating curve slope as equivalent to the absolute level of rates.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| 2-year Treasury yield | Official daily 2Y par/constant maturity yield. |
| 10-year Treasury yield | Official daily 10Y par/constant maturity yield. |
| 30-year Treasury yield | Official daily 30Y par/constant maturity yield. |
| 2s10s spread | (10Y yield - 2Y yield) x 100 basis points. |
| 10Y real yield | Official Treasury/FRED real yield series; no synthetic substitution if the official observation is unavailable. |
| High-yield OAS | Approved FRED credit-spread series. |
| Investment-grade OAS | Approved FRED credit-spread series. |
| Broad dollar / approved USD index | Use an approved public series; explicitly distinguish broad Fed dollar index from market DXY if both are shown. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** TLT, IEF, SHY, HYG, LQD, GLD
**Primary sources:** U.S. Treasury, FRED/ALFRED, approved public FX/market-data feed.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether financing conditions are loosening or tightening, and distinguish yield-curve shape from the absolute discount rate and credit spreads.
### V4 - Fiscal Position
**Core question:** Is the federal financing burden becoming more difficult, and are Treasury auctions showing weakening or strengthening demand?
**Purpose:** Track sovereign balance-sheet pressure and the ability of markets to absorb Treasury supply without conflating a large debt level with an immediate crisis.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| Debt held by the public | Latest Treasury Fiscal Data debt_held_public_amt. |
| Rolling-12-month deficit | Sum of the most recent 12 months of federal receipts minus outlays using Treasury/BEA-approved series; exact source and sign convention documented. |
| Rolling-12-month net interest | Sum of the most recent 12 months of net interest outlays from the approved fiscal source. |
| Net interest / receipts | Rolling-12-month net interest / rolling-12-month federal receipts. |
| Debt / GDP | Debt held by the public / nominal GDP, aligned to the latest comparable quarter; show vintage dates. |
| 10Y/30Y auction bid-to-cover | Published bid-to-cover. Also show deviation from trailing 12 comparable auctions: current minus median. |
| Indirect bidder share | Indirect bidder accepted / total accepted x 100 for comparable auctions, when available. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** TLT, IEF, SHY, GLD
**Primary sources:** Treasury Fiscal Data, TreasuryDirect auction query, BEA/FRED for GDP, CBO only as an external forecast/context source rather than an observed-data feed.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether debt-service costs and auction absorption are improving or deteriorating. Do not equate one weak auction with a regime change; use trailing comparisons.
### V5 - AI Productivity Diffusion
**Core question:** Is AI adoption translating into measurable productivity and investment gains outside a narrow group of AI suppliers?
**Purpose:** Provide the principal counterweight to a purely bearish AI thesis by testing whether lower AI costs create broad real-economy value.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| Business AI adoption | Census BTOS percentage of employer businesses reporting current AI use; show total, sector and firm-size views where available. |
| Expected AI adoption | BTOS share expecting AI use in the next six months. |
| Labor productivity | BLS nonfarm business labor productivity; show quarterly annualized and year-over-year where available. |
| Output per hour | BLS measure aligned with labor productivity reporting. |
| Software / IP investment | BEA real private fixed investment in software/intellectual property using documented table/line identifiers. |
| Information-processing equipment investment | BEA real investment series where available. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** SPY, RSP, XLK, XLI, IGV, IWM
**Primary sources:** Census BTOS, BLS productivity data, BEA NIPA/fixed-investment data.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether adoption is diffusing beyond large technology firms and whether productivity/investment measures are moving in the same direction. Avoid claiming causality from simple co-movement.
### V6 - Energy
**Core question:** Are power, fuel and grid constraints becoming a binding cost on AI infrastructure and the broader economy?
**Purpose:** Track the physical infrastructure side of the AI buildout and inflation-sensitive energy shocks separately from labor.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| U.S. electricity demand | Approved EIA hourly/daily demand series aggregated to a documented interval; show YoY comparison. |
| Commercial/industrial electricity price | EIA retail price series, YoY and trailing trend. |
| Henry Hub natural gas | Approved EIA/FRED/market series; document source and frequency. |
| Brent and WTI | Approved market/EIA series; distinguish spot/futures methodology. |
| Regional grid load | PJM/ERCOT or other approved public grid data for major data-center regions when feasible. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** XLE, XLU, XOP, CEG, VST
**Primary sources:** EIA and approved public ISO/RTO feeds; approved market-data source for tradeable instruments.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether energy demand and prices are raising the marginal cost of AI/data-center expansion or creating broader inflation pressure. Treat geopolitical attribution as evidence-based, not assumed.
### V7 - Labor
**Core question:** Is the labor market absorbing AI-related change while maintaining income and demand, or is weakness emerging in exposed sectors?
**Purpose:** Separate the labor-transition risk from productivity and energy, and track whether AI diffusion is associated with healthy adjustment or demand-damaging weakness.
| Metric | Calculation / definition |
| --- | --- |
| Unemployment rate | BLS headline unemployment rate with release/vintage status. |
| Nonfarm payroll change | Monthly payroll change with revisions retained. |
| Initial unemployment claims | Approved Department of Labor/FRED weekly series. |
| Job openings | BLS JOLTS openings level/rate. |
| Average weekly hours | BLS total private / relevant-sector hours. |
| Wage growth | Average hourly earnings YoY and/or ECI, source clearly labeled. |
| Sector employment | Information; Professional and Business Services; data-processing/hosting or other selected AI-exposed industries where robust series exist. |

**Relevant market tickers/instruments:** SPY, RSP, IWM, XLY, XLP
**Primary sources:** BLS, Department of Labor/FRED, approved market-data source.
**Interpretation requirement:** Explain whether labor demand is resilient, cooling, or deteriorating and whether weakness is broad or concentrated. Do not attribute sector changes to AI unless supporting evidence exists.

## 10. Calculation and interpretation transparency
The dashboard must treat the methodology registry as executable documentation. The same registry should drive calculation tooltips/dropdowns, the Definitions & Methodology page, automated tests and agent instructions. A formula cannot exist only in UI copy or only in code.
Every derived observation should carry at least: metric_id, value, unit, observation_date/time, retrieved_at, source_id, source_url/endpoint, raw input references, formula_version, revision/vintage metadata, quality flags and calculation status.
### 10.1 Interpretation engine
- Deterministic rule evaluation happens before any natural-language generation.
- Rules operate on observed levels, deltas, trend windows, freshness and confidence/quality flags.
- An optional LLM may compress the rule output into cleaner prose, but it receives structured facts only and cannot add facts.
- Every interpretation has a hidden/displayable "why" payload listing triggered rules and supporting observations.
- When signals conflict, the interpretation states the conflict. The system must not force a bullish/bearish label.
- Stale or missing critical inputs lower confidence and are mentioned explicitly.

## 11. Evidence framework
### 11.1 Evidence quality labels
| Label | Definition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRIMARY / VERIFIED | Original authoritative source or filing. | SEC filing; Treasury/Fed/BEA/BLS/Census/EIA release; company IR filing/press release. |
| SECONDARY / ESTIMATE | Credible reporting, analyst estimate, private-company figure or proxy that cannot be independently reconstructed from primary public data. | Reuters-reported private company metric; analyst forecast; platform-specific token usage share. |
| THESIS / CLAIM | Interpretation, prediction or hypothesis from Paulsen, Eisman or another commentator. | Yield-curve thesis; price-war warning; causal claim about corporate debt crowding Treasury demand. |

### 11.2 Evidence impact labels
| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| SUPPORTS | The observation moves in the direction predicted by the named hypothesis. |
| COUNTERS | The observation moves against the named hypothesis. |
| AMBIGUOUS | Evidence is mixed, mechanism is unclear, or data are insufficient to classify confidently. |

A single item may support one hypothesis and counter another. Store impact relative to a named target, not as a global attribute.

## 13. Architecture and data location
Recommended MVP architecture: private GitHub repository + GitHub Actions collectors + curated Parquet files + DuckDB analytical views + Streamlit Community Cloud. This is intentionally simple and portable.
```text
Official APIs / filings / market source / approved public feeds
                      |
              Python collectors
                      |
          raw metadata + curated Parquet
                      |
              DuckDB analytical layer
                      |
       calculations + provenance + rules
                      |
               Streamlit dashboard
                      |
          Dashboard / Evidence / About / Methodology
```

### 13.1 Where files live in MVP
- **GitHub private repo:** application code, tests, PRD, metric/source registry, small curated historical Parquet datasets, CI workflows.
- **GitHub Actions:** scheduled collectors write/update curated data and commit or publish approved artifacts. Never persist secrets in artifacts.
- **DuckDB:** embedded query/calculation engine; no separate database server or account. It reads Parquet and/or a local DuckDB file at runtime.
- **Streamlit Community Cloud:** hosts the Python application and pulls code from GitHub. Runtime disk is not treated as the system of record.
- **Future migration:** if data volume, concurrency, or update frequency outgrow repo-based storage, move raw/curated data to object storage and/or PostgreSQL without changing metric definitions or the app contract.

## 14. Data freshness, revision and failure policy

## 15. Security, privacy and operational controls

- Use GitHub Actions Secrets and Streamlit Secrets/environment variables.
- Use least-privilege GitHub permissions; workflows should default to read-only and request write access only where required.
- Add automated dependency and secret scanning.

## 17. Agent execution protocol
This section is intentionally operational so another LLM/agent can work autonomously without presenting incomplete work as finished.

### 17.1 Inter-agent coordination and shared state
**Default operating model:** ChatGPT Work (or an equivalent project coordinator) owns phase orchestration, research, acceptance criteria and owner-facing status. Codex (or an equivalent implementation agent) performs repository-level software work, tests and debugging. These roles may be performed by other tools, but the coordination contract remains the same.

**The repository is the shared state layer.** Work and Codex must not assume access to one another's private chat history. Routine handoffs must be persisted in repo/GitHub artifacts, including the active task, applicable requirement/test IDs, decisions, implementation notes, test results, verification status and blockers. Preferred artifacts are `docs/phase-status.md`, `docs/handoffs/current-task.md`, `docs/verification/<phase>-report.md`, commits/PRs, issues and CI artifacts.

**No-user-relay rule:** The owner is not a message bus. Do not require the owner to copy/paste prompts, implementation summaries, test results or context between Work and Codex. Ask the owner only for credentials/permissions, paid-service approval, unresolved product decisions, external-account actions, or blockers that genuinely require human judgment.

**Handoff protocol:** Before an implementation task starts, the coordinator writes or updates the repo-resident task artifact. After implementation, the implementation agent commits code/tests and writes the verification evidence/status back to the repo/GitHub. The coordinator then reviews those artifacts against the phase exit gate. Any failed gate returns to the implementation loop with a repo-resident task update.

| Step | Agent instruction |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Read before changing | Read this PRD, decision log, registry files and current phase verification report. Do not infer a product decision already specified here. |
| 2. State phase scope | Identify the active phase, exact deliverables and acceptance tests. Avoid opportunistic work from later phases unless required to unblock the current phase. |
| 3. Plan small changes | Break work into reversible commits/PRs. Each code change should map to one or more requirement/test IDs. |
| 4. Build | Implement source adapters, calculations, UI or tests using the approved architecture and cost gates. |
| 5. Self-assess | Run lint/type checks, unit tests, integration tests, reconciliation samples and UI smoke tests applicable to the phase. |
| 6. Fix failures | Do not waive a failing test unless the PRD explicitly allows an exception. Fix root cause, add regression test, rerun. |
| 7. Verify evidence | For data work, compare samples to original primary source. For UI work, capture/render screenshots. For formulas, show input/output examples. |
| 8. Produce phase report | Summarize changes, tests run, pass/fail results, source reconciliation, screenshots if relevant, known limitations and open blockers. |
| 9. Gate progression | Advance only when all phase exit criteria pass and no P0/P1 defect remains. If a decision/cost approval is required, stop and request it. |
| 10. Preserve provenance | Never change a formula, source, taxonomy or interpretation rule without versioning the registry/decision log and updating tests. |
| 11. Persist handoff state | Before ending a work unit, update the active task/phase-status and verification artifacts so a fresh agent can continue without private chat context or owner relay. |

## 20. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False precision | A composite risk score could imply unsupported certainty. | Do not create a master buy/sell score; show independent signals and contradictions. |
| Proxy quality for AI usage | Public token usage is sample-biased and platform-specific. | Label sample, preserve methodology, use multiple proxies where possible, never extrapolate silently. |
| Private company opacity | OpenAI/Anthropic financials may be incomplete or secondary. | Separate estimates from verified filings; do not infer missing margins. |
| Market-data licensing | Real-time exchange data can require paid entitlements. | Use delayed/EOD MVP data; paid source requires approval. |
| Macro revisions | Payroll/GDP/productivity data can be materially revised. | Retain vintages/revisions and backtest with then-known values. |
| SEC taxonomy variability | Company XBRL tags and definitions vary. | Company-specific mappings, reconciliations, and review flags. |
| Source outages / schema changes | Public APIs can change or fail. | Retries, contract tests, source adapters, last-good cache, health panel. |
| LLM overreach | Narrative generation may add unsupported causal claims. | Deterministic rules first; structured fact context; optional LLM only for rewriting; test hallucination/prompt injection. |
| Git repository bloat | Raw snapshots can make git too large. | Keep curated Parquet in repo initially; limit raw retention; graduate to object storage if thresholds are exceeded. |
| Overfitting to personalities | Product could become a Paulsen/Eisman fan/critic tool rather than an evidence system. | Hypotheses are starting lenses only; dashboard design centers on observable variables and counter-evidence. |
| Chat-history dependency | Work and Codex may have separate contexts; hidden handoff state can make builds non-reproducible and force the owner to relay messages. | Persist task state, phase status, decisions and verification results in repo/GitHub artifacts; enforce P0-T05 and FR-023. |
